Absolutely. I went through both handwritten pages and expanded them into a proper website requirements / design brief that you can directly give to your website designer or developer.
The overall goal should be: the website should not feel like a generic photography portfolio. It should feel premium, cinematic, artistic, emotional, and trustworthy — something that makes a potential client imagine themselves being photographed by the brand.
Photography & Cinematography Website — Detailed Requirement Brief
1. Overall Vision
We are building a premium website for a Photography & Cinematography company/brand.
The website should showcase our work in a visually impressive way while also converting visitors into enquiries.
The primary objectives are:
- Showcase our best photography and cinematography work.
- Introduce the photographer/company and team.
- Clearly show the different services we provide.
- Make it very easy for visitors to contact us.
- Allow visitors to submit their event date, city, event type and approximate budget through a poll of different budget range.
- Give the website owner/admin the ability to manage portfolio photographs without needing a developer every time.
- Keep the overall appearance elegant, premium and artistic.
- Make the website feel professional enough for weddings, premium personal events, brands and corporate clients.
The website must work exceptionally well on mobile phones, because most clients will probably discover the business through Instagram, WhatsApp or referrals and open the website from their phone.
2. Visual Style / Colour Theme
The main website colour theme should revolve around:
Peacock Blue + Cream / Ivory
The colours should feel premium rather than overly colourful.
For example:
- Deep Peacock Blue — main brand colour
- Cream / Ivory — background
- White — content sections
- Dark charcoal — text
- Optional subtle gold/beige accents for luxury feel
The website should use plenty of whitespace.
Avoid making the site look crowded.
Photography must remain the main visual element and easily scrollable for customers to review as portfolio
Animations should be suble incase used 
Typography should combine something handwritten/ curvy/cursive elegant/editorial for headings with a very readable font for normal text.
The feeling should be:
Elegant + Artistic + Cinematic + Modern + Warm + Premium
3. Homepage
The homepage is extremely important.
Within a few seconds, the visitor should understand:
Who we are → What we shoot → Our style → Why they should choose us → How they can contact us.
Hero Section
The first screen should contain one exceptional photograph or a cinematic visual.
Potential layout:
[Brand/Studio Name]
Short emotional statement such as:
Stories. Emotions. Moments preserved forever.

or
Photography & Films crafted around your story.

Below that:
Photography | Cinematography | Weddings | Events | Brands
Two buttons:
View Our Work
Check Availability
Alternatively, the hero can contain a short muted cinematic video/showreel.
It should NOT autoplay with loud audio.
4. Homepage Introduction
After the hero section, introduce:
The Company
Short explanation about:
- who we are
- what type of photography we specialise in
- our philosophy
- how we approach photography/cinematography
- what makes our work different
This should not sound like a corporate company profile.
Instead, it should communicate things such as:
- capturing real emotions
- natural storytelling
- cinematic compositions
- making clients comfortable
- documenting meaningful moments rather than just taking photographs
5. Photographer / Founder Introduction
There should be a dedicated section introducing the main photographer.
Include:

The goal is for clients to feel:
"I know who will be photographing us."

Photography is a very personal service, especially weddings and family events, so showing the person behind the camera builds trust.

15. Portfolio Gallery Experience
This part is extremely important.

Possible layouts:

- Full-width photographs
- Editorial magazine-style layouts
- Horizontal cinematic galleries
- Fullscreen image viewing
When a visitor clicks a photo, it should open larger.
Users should be able to navigate:

Preferably with swipe gestures on mobile.
Images should load progressively so that hundreds of portfolio images don't make the site slow.

37. Website Performance
Photography websites easily become extremely slow because images are huge.
The developer must implement:
- automatic image compression
- WebP/AVIF images
- multiple image sizes
- lazy loading
- thumbnail generation
- CDN/image optimisation if required
We should be able to eventually have hundreds of photographs (500+ images) without making the website painfully slow.
This is especially important for mobile users.

47. One Important Principle for the Designer
The website should never compete with the photographs.
Avoid:
- excessive animation
- extremely colourful backgrounds
- complicated UI
- dozens of buttons
- clutter
- giant blocks of text
- unnecessary effects
Every design decision should make the photography look better.
The desired reaction when someone opens the website should be:
"Wow. I want my photos to look like this."

rather than:
"Wow, that's a fancy website."

A one-line direction for the designer
You can give your designer this sentence as the core design philosophy:
"Create a premium, modern and cinematic photography experience using peacock blue and cream, where large emotional photographs and films dominate the design. The site should feel like an editorial luxury portfolio rather than a standard business website, while quietly guiding visitors toward checking availability and contacting us."

And one more thing I'd prioritise heavily for Crafted Media: don't launch with 500 photos visible just because you can store 500. Have hundreds available in the backend, but initially curate perhaps 40–80 exceptional images across the primary portfolios. A prospective client remembers 10 remarkable photographs far more than 200 average ones.
